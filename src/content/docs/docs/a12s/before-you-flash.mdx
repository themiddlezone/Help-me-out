Flashing a custom ROM replaces the software Samsung shipped. It is reversible, but it wipes the phone along the way
and a few things never come back. Read this page first.

## Make sure it is the right phone

These builds are for the **Samsung Galaxy A12 SM-A127F** only. That is the Exynos 850 model, codename `a12s`.

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings**, then **About phone**.
2. Look at **Model number**. It should start with `SM-A127F`. A suffix like `/DSN` is fine.

</Steps>

<Aside type="danger">
  If it says `SM-A125F` you have the MediaTek Galaxy A12. Nothing on this site supports it, and flashing these files on
  it can leave it unable to boot.
</Aside>

## What you will need

- A computer running Linux, macOS or Windows.
- A USB cable that can carry data, not only charge.
- [Android platform tools](https://developer.android.com/tools/releases/platform-tools) for `adb`.
- [Heimdall](https://git.sr.ht/~grimler/Heimdall) to write images to the phone from download mode. On Windows, Odin
  works too.
- At least 50% battery.
- About an hour, without rushing.

## What you lose

- **Everything on the phone.** Unlocking the bootloader and formatting data both wipe it.
- **Knox.** Unlocking the bootloader trips Samsung's Knox warranty flag for good. Samsung Wallet, Secure Folder and
  some Samsung Health features stop working, even if you go back to stock later.
- **Some apps.** Apps that check device integrity, such as some banking and payment apps, may refuse to run on an
  unlocked phone.

<Aside type="caution" title="Encryption and SELinux">
  Check the **SELinux** and **Userdata** rows in a release's notes before you pick it. Release 1 runs SELinux in
  permissive mode and leaves user data unencrypted, which makes the phone less private if it is lost. Newer test builds
  turn on encryption and enforcing mode.
</Aside>

## Back up

Copy what you want to keep before you start:

- Photos, videos and downloads: plug the phone into your computer and copy the folders off, or use a cloud backup.
- Contacts, calendars and app data: let your Google account or Samsung account sync them.
- Chats: most messaging apps have their own backup option in their settings. Run it.
- Two-factor apps: move them to another device first. Losing these is the most painful part of a wipe.

When that is done, move on to [unlocking the bootloader](../unlock-bootloader/).
