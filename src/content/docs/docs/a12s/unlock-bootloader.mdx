---

The bootloader decides what the phone is allowed to boot. Samsung ships it locked, and it has to be unlocked before
anything custom will run.

<Aside type="danger" title="This wipes the phone">
  Unlocking erases everything on the device and trips Knox. Finish [Before you flash](../before-you-flash/) first.
</Aside>

## Turn on OEM unlocking

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings**, then **About phone**, then **Software information**.
2. Tap **Build number** seven times. Enter your PIN if asked. Developer options are now on.
3. Go back to **Settings** and open **Developer options** at the bottom.
4. Turn on **OEM unlocking** and confirm.

</Steps>

<Aside type="tip" title="No OEM unlocking switch?">
  Connect to Wi-Fi, check for a system update and give the phone some time online. On some firmware the switch only
  shows up after the phone has checked in with Samsung.
</Aside>

## Unlock from download mode

<Steps>

1. Turn the phone off.
2. Hold **Volume up** and **Volume down** together, and while holding them plug the phone into your computer. A
   blue warning screen appears.
3. **Long press Volume up** to open device unlock mode.
4. Read the screen, then press **Volume up** once more to confirm. The phone wipes itself and restarts.

</Steps>

## Check it held

<Steps>

1. Go through the setup screens quickly. You can skip the accounts, but **connect to the internet**.
2. Turn on Developer options again, as above.
3. Open Developer options and check that **OEM unlocking** is on and greyed out, with a note that the bootloader is
   already unlocked.

</Steps>

If the switch is missing after the unlock, stay online for a while and check again before you flash anything. Flashing
while the phone still treats itself as locked can leave it refusing to boot until you restore stock firmware.
